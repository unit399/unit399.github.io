Retrospective formats

Every retrospective format in AgileMate, with its steps and timings. Free to read, free to run.

57 formats

Sailboat

A visual metaphor retro: the wind pushes the team forward, anchors hold it back, rocks are risks ahead, and the island is the goal. Great for teams that think better in pictures.

60 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

4L

Four lists — Liked, Learned, Lacked, Longed for — give a balanced view of the sprint. Simple to explain and works for almost any team.

60 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Starfish

Five arms — Keep, More of, Less of, Start, Stop — push the team beyond good/bad thinking into dosage: what deserves more, what deserves less.

60 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Mad / Sad / Glad

An emotions-first retro: what made us mad, sad, or glad. Great for new teams learning to talk about feelings safely.

45 min · 3–9 people · Remote or in person

Start / Stop / Continue

The classic action-oriented retro: what should we start doing, stop doing, and continue doing. Fast, concrete, hard to get wrong.

45 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Lean Coffee

No agenda until the meeting starts: the team proposes topics, votes, and discusses them in strict timeboxes, thumb-voting to continue or move on. Democratic and energizing.

60 min · 3–8 people · Remote or in person

KALM

Keep, Add, Less, More — a calm dosage conversation about existing practices rather than dramatic change. Good for stable teams fine-tuning.

45 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

DAKI

Drop, Add, Keep, Improve — a decisive retro that forces clear choices about practices: what dies, what's born, what stays, what evolves.

45 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Plus / Delta

The minimal retro: what worked (+) and what we'd change (Δ). Two columns, thirty minutes, zero setup — ideal when time is short.

30 min · 2–12 people · Remote or in person

Speed Car

The team is a race car: the engine drives us, the parachute drags us, the abyss ahead is the risk, the bridge is how we cross it.

45 min · 3–8 people · Remote or in person

Hot Air Balloon

Hot air lifts us, sandbags weigh us down, the storm ahead threatens, sunny skies are where we want to fly. Adds a forward-looking twist to the classic metaphor retro.

60 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Three Little Pigs

House of straw (fragile), house of sticks (okay-ish), house of bricks (solid) — plus the wolf: what could blow us over. A playful way to audit practices.

60 min · 3–9 people · In person

Superhero

The team as a superhero: superpowers, kryptonite, arch-nemesis, and trusty sidekick. Reveals identity and dependencies with humor — best with teams that know each other.

60 min · 4–10 people · In person

Genie in a Bottle

The genie grants three wishes: one for the product, one for the process, one for the team. Wishes reveal what people truly miss — then get grounded into actions.

30 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Movie Critic

Review the sprint as a film: title and genre, star rating, best scene, scene that should have been cut, and what the sequel needs. Light-hearted but surprisingly revealing.

45 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Weather Report

Everyone marks the sprint's weather — sunny, cloudy, rainy, stormy — then forecasts the next one. A gentle, low-pressure format for teams new to retros.

30 min · 2–12 people · Remote or in person

5 Whys

Pick the sprint's most painful problem and ask 'why' five times until the root cause appears. Narrow but deep — best when one issue clearly dominates.

45 min · 3–8 people · Remote or in person

Fishbone

An Ishikawa diagram for one stubborn problem: causes sorted along bones like people, process, tools, environment. Structured root-cause hunting for recurring issues.

75 min · 4–10 people · Remote or in person

Circles and Soup

Three rings — we control, we influence, the soup we swim in. Sorts frustrations by agency and turns each ring into a different kind of response. Antidote to helplessness.

60 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Pre-mortem

Imagine the next sprint has failed spectacularly — now explain why. Prospective hindsight surfaces risks people hesitate to name directly. Run it before risky sprints.

60 min · 4–10 people · Remote or in person

Six Thinking Hats

The whole team wears one thinking mode at a time: facts, feelings, risks, benefits, ideas, and process. Prevents debate ping-pong and gives quiet voices structure.

75 min · 4–10 people · Remote or in person

Timeline

Reconstruct the sprint on a long wall: events, decisions, surprises, and how people felt at each point. The richest picture of what actually happened — worth the longer timebox.

90 min · 4–12 people · In person

Emotional Seismograph

Everyone draws their personal energy line across the sprint, then the lines are overlaid. Shared dips and peaks point straight at what needs discussing.

45 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Team Battery

Everyone marks their charge level, then the team collects what charges and what drains the battery. A fast energy check that ends with one recharge action.

30 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

One Word

Each person sums up the sprint in exactly one word, then the words are explored. The fastest retro there is — perfect as a check-in or when time collapsed.

15 min · 3–12 people · Remote or in person

ESVP

Anonymous vote: is everyone here an Explorer, Shopper, Vacationer, or Prisoner? Measures engagement with the retro itself and opens an honest conversation about it.

20 min · 4–12 people · Remote or in person

Rose, Bud, Thorn

Roses bloomed (wins), buds are promising (opportunities), thorns hurt (problems). The bud column makes this gentler and more forward-looking than most three-column retros.

45 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

WRAP

Wishes, Risks, Appreciations, Puzzles — a balanced mix of hopes, dangers, gratitude, and open questions. Appreciations make it a warm mid-project pulse check.

45 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Remember the Future

Jump ahead: the release shipped and it was a triumph. Looking back from that future, what did we do to get there? Back-casting makes bold plans feel concrete.

75 min · 4–10 people · Remote or in person

Team Radar

The team picks 5–6 dimensions it cares about — like quality, focus, fun, courage — rates each, and plots a radar. Gaps between ratings spark the honest conversations.

60 min · 3–12 people · Remote or in person

Appreciation Retro

A retro dedicated to gratitude: everyone writes and hands appreciation cards to teammates. Run it after hard sprints or releases — trust is a deliverable too.

30 min · 3–12 people · In person

Async Board

The team fills the board over a day, then meets briefly to decide. For teams that are never all awake at once — the call is spent on decisions, not on collecting input.

30 min · 3–12 people · Remote

Signal Check

Twenty minutes on how connected the team felt, not on what it shipped. Distance is not only kilometres, and a distributed team loses the signal long before anyone mentions it.

20 min · 3–12 people · Remote

Handoff Map

Trace the last few pieces of work end to end and mark every point where they changed hands. Across time zones a handoff costs a day that never shows up on the board.

45 min · 4–12 people · Remote

Round Robin

A strict rotation so the fastest microphone stops winning. On a call the quiet half of the team disappears unless the format makes room for it.

45 min · 3–9 people · Remote

Tooling Tax

A distributed team lives inside its tools and pays the rent in minutes. Thirty minutes to find where the tools cost more than they give.

30 min · 3–12 people · Remote

One Team, Many Places

A distributed team quietly splits into smaller ones. This retro finds the seam — a location, a time zone, a channel, a role — and moves it a little.

45 min · 4–12 people · Remote

Board Walk

Walk the board right to left, one card at a time, and let the work tell you where it got stuck. For teams whose problems live between the columns rather than inside them.

45 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

WIP Honesty

Count what each person is really carrying, including the things not on the board. Uncomfortable and short — for teams that finish nothing while everyone is busy.

30 min · 3–9 people · Remote or in person

Aging Work

Sort the in-progress cards by how long they have been sitting there, and talk only about the oldest. Short, blunt, and hard to argue with.

30 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Queue of One

Follow a single item from request to delivered and mark every wait. The most reliable way to show a team that its delays are not where it thinks.

60 min · 4–12 people · Remote or in person

The Blocked Column

Give every impediment of the last few weeks a name, an owner and a decision. For teams that raise the same blockers in every standup and resolve none of them.

45 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

The Late Dependency

Put two dates on every cross-team dependency — when you found it and when you needed it — and go looking for the signal you already had. For groups that keep learning about the other team's work halfway through their own.

60 min · 5–12 people · Remote or in person

The Handover

Sit both teams in front of the work that crosses between them and mark the moment it stopped being anybody's. For two teams whose items sit untouched for days after one side considers them delivered.

45 min · 4–10 people · Remote or in person

The Shared Queue

Put every team's open requests to one shared group on a single wall and look at them with that group in the room. For the platform, data or ops team everyone is waiting on and nobody wants to blame.

60 min · 5–12 people · Remote or in person

Agreed, Not Done

Take the cross-team decisions of the last few weeks and go looking for them in the actual work. For groups that leave meetings agreeing with each other and find out months later that nothing changed.

45 min · 4–10 people · Remote or in person

Who Can Say Yes

Test the room against the changes it wants before spending an hour on things nobody present can decide. For cross-team retros that produce good conversation and no movement.

40 min · 6–12 people · Remote or in person

Assumption Audit

Write down what the team believed when it started each piece of work, then check which of those beliefs was ever tested. For teams that ship on time and find out months later that the premise was wrong.

60 min · 3–9 people · Remote or in person

Learned or Assumed

Read out what the team knows about its users and count how much of it anyone present has heard first-hand. For teams whose picture of the user is old enough to be wrong.

45 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Feature Graveyard

Walk through what the team shipped over the last few months and find the parts nobody uses. For teams that only ever add — every unused feature is paid for again in every release.

45 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person

Output and Outcome

Put everything the team finished next to what actually changed for the people using it. For teams with a full done column and no way to tell whether any of it mattered.

60 min · 4–10 people · Remote or in person

The Standing Decision

Find the choice the team made once, long ago, and has been building on ever since without checking. Short and specific — for teams whose constraints are older than their product.

30 min · 3–9 people · Remote or in person

Timeline Before Theory

Rebuild what happened as a list of facts everyone agrees on, and refuse to discuss causes until the list is finished. For teams whose incident reviews turn into an argument in the first ten minutes because two people are describing different incidents.

60 min · 4–10 people · Remote or in person

The Detection Gap

Measure the distance between the moment the system broke and the moment a human knew, then spend the whole session on that number. For teams that repair incidents quickly and still lose a day to them.

45 min · 3–9 people · Remote or in person

Contributing Conditions

Replace the hunt for one root cause with the list of conditions that all had to hold at the same time. For teams whose reviews stop at the last change anyone made and never reach the reasons that change was so easy to get wrong.

75 min · 4–12 people · Remote or in person

What On-Call Needed

Replay the incident from the position of whoever was holding the pager and list everything they had to hunt for, guess, or wake somebody up to ask. For teams that keep hardening the system and never improve the twenty minutes it costs someone at three in the morning.

45 min · 3–8 people · Remote or in person

The Near Miss Ledger

Collect the times something nearly went wrong and was quietly caught, before one of them stops being caught. For teams with a clean incident record and a quantity of luck they have never counted.

30 min · 3–10 people · Remote or in person
